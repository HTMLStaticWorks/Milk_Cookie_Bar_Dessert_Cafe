The Secret to Our 24-Hour Brown Butter Dough

EC
Elena Cookie May 15, 2024 • 8 min read
Recipes Technique

It’s the question we get asked most often at the bar: “Why do your cookies have that specific, deep, almost toffee-like flavor?” The answer isn't a secret ingredient—it's a secret process.

While most bakeries use creamed butter at room temperature, we take an extra step that changes the molecular structure of the cookie itself. We brown our butter until it reaches a deep amber hue, releasing those toasted, nutty aromas that are the hallmark of our signature chip.

Why 24 Hours Matters

Patience is the most difficult ingredient to master. Once our dough is mixed, it doesn't go straight into the oven. Instead, it "ages" in our walk-in cooler for a full day. This resting period allows the flour to fully hydrate and the fats to solidify, resulting in a cookie that spreads less and has a more complex, developed flavor.

"A cookie baked immediately is just a sweet treat. A cookie aged for 24 hours is a masterpiece of chemistry."

During those 24 hours, the enzymes in the flour break down the starches into simple sugars. This means when the cookie finally hits the oven, it caramelizes faster and more deeply, giving you those crispy, lacey edges and a soft, fudgy center.

Try It At Home

If you're baking this weekend, try this: browning your butter and then letting it cool back to a solid state before creaming it with your sugars. Then, once your dough is made, scoop it into balls and let them sit in the fridge overnight. The difference in texture and flavor will surprise you.
